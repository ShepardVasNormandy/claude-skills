# Deck outline

Target 8 to 12 slides plus an appendix. Drop a slide when the source has nothing for it and the gap does not matter. Keep the order: the board reads the ask first and the detail last.

| # | Slide | What it must say | Typical visual |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title and the ask | Title, presenter, date, and the decision asked in one sentence: "Approve building partial refunds in the order service for Q3." | None |
| 2 | Decision needed | What the board decides today, the options on the table, and what happens if it decides nothing. A deadline if there is one. | Short list |
| 3 | Context | The problem and why now, with one or two numbers from the source. | One stat or a small trend chart |
| 4 | Goals and non-goals | What success looks like, and what is out of scope. | Two-column list |
| 5 | Options | Each option in one line, including "do nothing" when it is real. | Table |
| 6 | Trade-offs | Options compared on the criteria that matter: cost, time, risk, fit with the current system. | Comparison table or matrix |
| 7 | Recommendation | The chosen option and the two or three reasons that decide it. | Highlighted row or short list |
| 8 | How it works | The proposed design at a level the board can follow. | One diagram |
| 9 | Risks and mitigations | Top 3 to 5 risks, likelihood, impact and the mitigation for each. | Table or risk matrix |
| 10 | Cost and effort | People, weeks, running cost, and what gets delayed to make room. Ranges are fine; say where they come from. | Table or bar chart |
| 11 | Rollout | Phases, flags, migration, rollback and checkpoints where the board hears back. | Timeline |
| 12 | Open questions | What is not settled, with an owner for each. Questions the board must answer come first. | List |
| A | Appendix | Detailed alternatives, data, benchmarks, data model, API changes, links to the RFC. | As needed |

## Writing the slides

- Slide titles are full sentences that state the point.
- Every number has a unit and a source note.
- Speaker notes carry the source reference (file and section, or Notion page and heading) for each claim on the slide.
- Use the same option names on every slide ("A: patch the monolith", "B: separate refund service").
- Present the options fairly before the recommendation. A board that sees only one real option will ask for the others.
